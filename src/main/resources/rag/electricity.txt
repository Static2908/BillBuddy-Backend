BILLBUDDY ELECTRICITY BILLING KNOWLEDGE BASE

==================================================
1. ELECTRICITY USAGE
==================================================

Electricity consumption is commonly measured in kilowatt-hours (kWh).

A kilowatt-hour represents the amount of energy consumed by using
one kilowatt of power for one hour.

Example:

Usage = 250 kWh

This means the recorded electricity consumption for the billing
period is 250 kilowatt-hours.


==================================================
2. ELECTRICITY BILL AMOUNT
==================================================

The total electricity bill may contain more than the charge directly
associated with electricity consumption.

Depending on the billing system, a bill may include:

- Energy or consumption charges.
- Fixed charges.
- Service charges.
- Taxes.
- Regulatory charges.
- Other applicable fees.
- Billing adjustments.

The exact composition of a bill depends on the electricity provider
and the applicable billing rules.

BillBuddy should use the actual bill information when available rather
than assuming a particular charge exists.


==================================================
3. ELECTRICITY USAGE INCREASE
==================================================

An increase in electricity usage means that more electrical energy was
recorded during the current billing period compared with a previous
period.

Possible contributing factors include:

- Increased use of electrical appliances.
- Longer operating time of appliances.
- Additional electrical devices.
- Seasonal changes.
- Changes in occupancy or daily activity.
- Changes in usage behavior.

These are possible explanations and should not be treated as confirmed
causes without supporting evidence.


==================================================
4. ELECTRICITY BILL AMOUNT INCREASE
==================================================

An electricity bill can increase because of:

- Increased electricity consumption.
- Changes in applicable rates or tariffs.
- Fixed charges.
- Taxes or regulatory charges.
- Additional fees.
- Billing adjustments.
- Changes in the billing period.

A higher bill amount does not automatically mean that electricity
consumption increased.

BillBuddy should compare both the total amount and usage before
interpreting an electricity bill increase.


==================================================
5. COST PER UNIT
==================================================

For BillBuddy analysis, electricity cost per unit can be calculated as:

Cost per unit = Total bill amount / Electricity usage in kWh

Example:

Total bill = 3000
Usage = 300 kWh

Cost per unit = 3000 / 300
              = 10 per kWh

Comparing cost per unit across billing periods can help identify
whether the amount paid changed disproportionately to electricity
consumption.

This calculated value is an analytical measure and may not represent
the official tariff rate because the total bill may contain fixed
charges, taxes, fees, or other components.


==================================================
6. ELECTRICITY BILL ANOMALIES
==================================================

BillBuddy may detect the following electricity-related anomalies:

AMOUNT_SPIKE

A significant increase in the total bill amount compared with the
previous electricity bill.

BillBuddy threshold:
20 percent or greater increase.


USAGE_SPIKE

A significant increase in electricity consumption compared with the
previous electricity bill.

BillBuddy threshold:
20 percent or greater increase.


COST_PER_UNIT_SPIKE

A significant increase in calculated cost per kWh compared with the
previous electricity bill.

BillBuddy threshold:
15 percent or greater increase.


ZERO_USAGE_NONZERO_AMOUNT

The bill contains zero recorded electricity usage while the total
amount is greater than zero.

Possible explanations may include fixed charges, minimum charges,
taxes, fees, adjustments, or missing/incorrect usage information.

The system should not assume that the electricity bill is incorrect
without additional evidence.


==================================================
7. INTERPRETING ELECTRICITY BILL CHANGES
==================================================

BillBuddy should compare:

1. Previous electricity bill amount.
2. Current electricity bill amount.
3. Previous electricity usage.
4. Current electricity usage.
5. Previous cost per kWh.
6. Current cost per kWh.

Example:

Previous:
Amount = 2500
Usage = 250 kWh
Cost per unit = 10

Current:
Amount = 3000
Usage = 300 kWh
Cost per unit = 10

Interpretation:

The bill amount increased by 20 percent and electricity usage also
increased by 20 percent.

The calculated cost per unit remained unchanged.

Therefore, the available data indicates that the increase in the bill
amount corresponds with the increase in recorded electricity usage.

It does not establish why the electricity usage increased.


==================================================
8. WHEN ELECTRICITY USAGE DOES NOT INCREASE
==================================================

If the electricity usage remains approximately the same but the total
bill amount increases significantly, BillBuddy should consider the
following as possible explanations:

- Changes in applicable charges.
- Fixed charges.
- Taxes or fees.
- Billing adjustments.
- Rate or tariff changes.

However, BillBuddy must not claim that any specific factor caused
the increase unless supporting information is available.


==================================================
9. ELECTRICITY BILL VERIFICATION
==================================================

When an electricity bill appears unusual, the user may verify:

- Recorded electricity usage.
- Billing period.
- Previous meter reading.
- Current meter reading.
- Applicable charges.
- Fixed charges.
- Taxes and fees.
- Billing adjustments.
- Provider-issued tariff information.

BillBuddy should provide these as verification suggestions rather than
claiming that an error definitely occurred.


==================================================
10. EVIDENCE RULE
==================================================

BillBuddy explanations must distinguish between:

FACT:
Information directly available from the bill data.

INFERENCE:
A conclusion calculated from available bill information.

POSSIBLE CAUSE:
A potential explanation that requires additional evidence.

Example:

FACT:
Electricity usage increased from 250 kWh to 300 kWh.

INFERENCE:
Usage increased by 20 percent.

POSSIBLE CAUSE:
Increased appliance usage could contribute to the increase.

The possible cause must not be presented as a confirmed explanation
unless supporting evidence is available.
