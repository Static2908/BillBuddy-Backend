BILLBUDDY BILLING ANOMALIES KNOWLEDGE BASE

==================================================
1. AMOUNT SPIKE
==================================================

Rule:
AMOUNT_SPIKE

Definition:
An amount spike occurs when the current bill amount increases significantly
compared with the previous bill for the same utility.

BillBuddy currently considers an increase of 20 percent or more to be
a significant amount increase.

Possible contributing factors include:

- Increased utility consumption.
- Changes in applicable tariffs or rates.
- Fixed charges or service charges.
- Taxes and regulatory charges.
- Additional fees.
- Billing adjustments.
- Previous-period corrections.
- Changes in the billing period.

Important:
BillBuddy must not claim that one of these factors definitely caused
the increase unless supporting information is available in the bill data.

Example:
Previous bill amount: 2500
Current bill amount: 3000
Increase: 500
Percentage increase: 20%

Interpretation:
The current bill is classified as having an amount spike because the
amount increased by 20 percent.


==================================================
2. USAGE SPIKE
==================================================

Rule:
USAGE_SPIKE

Definition:
A usage spike occurs when the current utility consumption increases
significantly compared with the previous bill for the same utility.

BillBuddy currently considers an increase of 20 percent or more to be
a significant usage increase.

Examples of usage units:

- Electricity: kWh
- Water: kL
- Internet: GB

Possible contributing factors include:

- Increased consumption.
- Changes in usage behavior.
- Seasonal changes.
- Increased number of devices or appliances.
- Longer operating or usage periods.
- Changes in household or user activity.

Important:
These are possible explanations only. BillBuddy must not present any
possible cause as a confirmed cause without supporting evidence.


==================================================
3. COST PER UNIT SPIKE
==================================================

Rule:
COST_PER_UNIT_SPIKE

Definition:
A cost-per-unit spike occurs when the amount paid for each unit of
consumption increases significantly compared with the previous bill.

BillBuddy currently considers an increase of 15 percent or more in
cost per unit to be significant.

Formula:

Cost per unit = Total bill amount / Usage amount

Percentage change:

((Current cost per unit - Previous cost per unit)
 / Previous cost per unit) × 100

Example:

Previous bill:
Amount = 3000
Usage = 300 kWh
Cost per unit = 10

Current bill:
Amount = 3900
Usage = 300 kWh
Cost per unit = 13

Cost per unit increase = 30%

Interpretation:
The cost per unit increased significantly even though the usage
remained the same.

Possible contributing factors include:

- Tariff or rate changes.
- Fixed or additional charges.
- Taxes or fees.
- Billing adjustments.
- Changes in the composition of charges.

Important:
BillBuddy must not state that a tariff change or another specific
factor occurred unless supporting information is available.


==================================================
4. ZERO USAGE WITH NON-ZERO AMOUNT
==================================================

Rule:
ZERO_USAGE_NONZERO_AMOUNT

Definition:
This anomaly occurs when a bill records zero usage while still
containing a non-zero bill amount.

Example:

Usage = 0
Bill amount = 1500

Possible explanations include:

- Fixed charges.
- Minimum charges.
- Taxes or fees.
- Service charges.
- Billing adjustments.
- Incorrect or missing usage information.
- Previous-period adjustments.

Recommended verification:

The user should check the detailed bill, charge breakdown,
meter or usage reading, and any applicable fixed charges.

Important:
BillBuddy must not assume that the bill is incorrect merely because
usage is zero and the amount is non-zero.


==================================================
5. COMBINED ANOMALIES
==================================================

A bill can contain more than one anomaly.

For example:

- AMOUNT_SPIKE
- USAGE_SPIKE

may occur together when both the bill amount and utility consumption
increase significantly.

Another example:

- AMOUNT_SPIKE
- COST_PER_UNIT_SPIKE

may occur when the bill amount increases substantially and the amount
paid per unit also increases.

BillBuddy should explain each detected anomaly separately and then
provide an overall interpretation.

The presence of multiple anomalies does not prove a specific cause.


==================================================
6. ANOMALY INTERPRETATION PRINCIPLES
==================================================

BillBuddy should follow these principles when explaining anomalies:

1. Report detected facts first.
2. Use the previous and current bill values as evidence.
3. Clearly distinguish facts from possible explanations.
4. Do not invent causes.
5. Do not claim tariff changes without tariff data.
6. Do not claim meter problems without supporting evidence.
7. Do not claim fraud, leakage, equipment failure, or provider error
   without supporting evidence.
8. Recommend reasonable verification steps when appropriate.
9. Keep explanations understandable to students.
10. Use the detected anomaly rule as the primary basis for the explanation.


==================================================
7. SUPPORTED BILLBUDDY ANOMALY THRESHOLDS
==================================================

AMOUNT_SPIKE:
20 percent or greater increase.

USAGE_SPIKE:
20 percent or greater increase.

COST_PER_UNIT_SPIKE:
15 percent or greater increase.

ZERO_USAGE_NONZERO_AMOUNT:
Usage is zero while the bill amount is greater than zero.


==================================================
8. EVIDENCE REQUIREMENT
==================================================

Every anomaly explanation should be grounded in available bill data.

Useful evidence includes:

- Previous bill amount.
- Current bill amount.
- Previous usage.
- Current usage.
- Usage unit.
- Previous cost per unit.
- Current cost per unit.
- Billing period.
- Utility type.
- Provider name.
- Detected anomaly rule.

If the available data does not establish a cause, the explanation
should explicitly say that the cause cannot be determined from the
available bill information.
